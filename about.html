About

I would rather you were comfortable than impressed

I trained in Kathmandu and have practised general dentistry here for nine years. Most of what I see is not dramatic: a tooth that has been getting worse for a year while somebody was working up the courage to mention it.

That pattern shaped how I work. I would rather spend the first ten minutes of an appointment on an examination and an explanation than on drilling. People who know what is happening in their own mouth tolerate a great deal more of it.

A large share of my patients were nervous patients first, and patients second. The pace of an appointment is theirs to set. Some people manage one drill and need to leave. I would far rather split the work across three visits than rush one and have you never come back.

I keep to general dentistry: fillings, root canals, crowns, gum treatment and the preventive care that makes the rest unnecessary. For anything involving surgery, orthodontics or very complex cases I refer on, and I will tell you that in the first appointment rather than the fifth.

Outside the practice I am usually somewhere in the Valley with a camera, or reading about restorative work that I do not yet have the equipment for.

In the chair

What you can expect

01

The first ten minutes are talking

What is bothering you, what you have tried, what worries you. Then an examination with your mouth open for about five minutes.

02

I show you what I found

Photos on the screen, in plain language, with what it would cost to fix and what happens if you leave it a while longer.

03

You decide the order

Urgent work first, cosmetic work later, and often not at all. A written plan goes home with you either way.

04

Your hand stops everything

Raise it and I stop. No comment, no persuasion. Most of my nervous patients need this agreed on before we begin.
