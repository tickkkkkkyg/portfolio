What your toothpaste is actually doing

There is one ingredient doing almost all of the work on that shelf. Everything else is largely marketing with a modest amount of benefit mixed in.

Ask me what toothpaste to buy and I will ask you one question: how much do you want to spend? That is not evasion. It is because the answer changes little, and the thing that changes everything is a habit I will come back to below.

Fluoride is the ingredient that matters

Fluoride does two things. It slows the breakdown of enamel in the acid that bacteria produce, and it helps remineralise the surface where early damage has started. Between them, those two effects are what a fluoride toothpaste is for. If a product is cheap and contains fluoride, it will do the job.

What you want is around 1350 to 1500 ppm fluoride. It should be on the label. If it is not listed with a number, that is a reasonable question for the shop.

Whitening toothpaste mostly does not

Most whitening pastes contain abrasives that remove surface stain. They can technically whiten teeth, because enamel is slightly yellow and you are sanding a thin layer off it. Over years this is not something I want you doing daily. A proper whitening course, which I can arrange, is a controlled version of the same idea.

Charcoal is a marketing category

Charcoal pastes are abrasive. They will not meaningfully change the colour of your teeth, they can make sensitive teeth worse, and they strip the enamel surface that a fluoride toothpaste is trying to protect. I would rather you spent the money on a better electric brush.

The habit beats the product

The most effective thing in this whole article is brushing twice a day for two minutes, and cleaning between the teeth once a day. Interdental brushes are far more effective than floss for most people, and far easier to actually do. This matters more than which brand is in the tube.

If your teeth are sensitive when you drink something cold, tell me at an appointment. It is usually fixable, and the fix is often a different toothpaste rather than a different treatment.
