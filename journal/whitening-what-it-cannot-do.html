Whitening: what it cannot do

It works well on tea and coffee stains and does nothing at all on fillings, crowns or veneers. Knowing which you have saves you money and disappointment.

The single most common misunderstanding about whitening is that it changes the colour of teeth. It does not. It removes stains from the outside of the enamel, and enamel underneath is a slightly warmer shade than most people picture.

What it does well

Tea, coffee, tobacco and red wine staining, and the general yellowing that comes with age. A take-home course with custom trays will shift most of this by several shades. It is the most predictable cosmetic treatment in dentistry.

What it does nothing for

Fillings, crowns and veneers do not whiten. If you have a white filling in a front tooth and whiten around it, that filling now stands out against the rest and looks worse than it did before. This is the most common way people end up unhappy with whitening.

Brown or grey discolouration also responds poorly. That usually comes from an old root-treated tooth or from tetracycline staining, and it needs a different approach, or none.

In the chair versus at home

In-chair whitening is faster and more dramatic in one afternoon, and more sensitive afterwards. Take-home trays take longer, cost considerably less, and are gentler on the enamel. For most people I recommend the trays. The main risk with both is sensitivity, which normally settles within a week of finishing.

What I will not do

I will not start whitening without first dealing with existing decay or leaking fillings. Bleaching can make a sensitive tooth considerably worse, and it is better to find that problem before it costs you more.

If you are considering whitening and have any visible fillings or crowns in your front teeth, mention it when you book. It changes what I would recommend, and occasionally it means whitening is not the right thing to spend money on.
