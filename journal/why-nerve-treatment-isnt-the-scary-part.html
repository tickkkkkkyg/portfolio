Root canal treatment is not the scary part

The reputation is thirty years out of date. Here is what it actually feels like, hour by hour.

Almost nobody is afraid of the treatment itself. People are afraid of the stories, which are usually second-hand and describe instruments from an era that no longer exists. So, hour by hour.

Hour one: numbing, and waiting

You sit back, I put a small rubber sheet over the tooth to keep everything dry and out of the way, then anaesthetic goes in. It is a brief pinch and then nothing. Most people wait about ten minutes for it to work, and I will not start until you tell me the tooth feels completely asleep. Testing it matters, because half-treated teeth are the reason people remember this as painful.

The part that feels strange

This is honest: you will feel pressure and you will hear a noise. Neither is pain. The rubber dam removes water, saliva and most of the sensation, and after ten minutes most people genuinely forget they are at the dentist.

Hour one to two: cleaning and sealing

The nerve is removed, the canal is cleaned and shaped, and it is sealed. That is it. Many teeth are finished in a single appointment of ninety minutes.

Then the part people did not expect

A tooth that has had a root canal becomes hollow and brittle. It very often needs a crown afterwards, and this is a second appointment. In my experience this is the part people find more tiresome than the treatment itself, because it involves an impression and a temporary crown and a wait. I would rather you knew that in advance than discovered it afterwards.

What it is genuinely like

Boring. That is the accurate description. It is a long, quiet appointment where nothing dramatic happens, and you leave with a tooth that no longer hurts. Compare that with the alternative, which is a tooth that does hurt, at three in the morning, until something is done.

If you have been told you need a root canal and have not had it, ask what happens if you wait. Sometimes the honest answer is that a tooth with a bad nerve will eventually break, and an extraction at that point is a longer and harder appointment than the one you are avoiding now.
