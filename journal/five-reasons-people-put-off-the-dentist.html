Five reasons people put off the dentist

Almost nobody avoids dental care because they do not care about their teeth. The reasons are practical, and most of them have a smaller answer than you would expect.

I ask every new patient why they are there, and almost nobody says they put off the dentist because they decided not to care. Here is what they actually say.

1. It costs more than you expect

Fair, and often true, because people compare a single appointment against a pharmacy bill rather than against the cost of an extraction later. Ask for an itemised estimate before you commit. Ask what happens if you wait a year. Most problems cost more and hurt more when left, and a small filling today avoids a root canal later.

2. A previous appointment went badly

The most common reason, and the one I can do least about retrospectively. What I can do is make sure you are in control of the next one: agree a stop signal before anything starts, and know that raising your hand pauses everything immediately. You never have to explain yourself twice.

3. It hurts, so you avoid confirming it

The fear that what you suspect is worse than what is real. It is often the other way round. An examination takes about five minutes with your mouth open, and in nine years I have very rarely found something that could not be dealt with calmly.

4. It does not hurt, so it does not count

This is the one that catches people out. Decay and gum disease are quiet for years, then they are not. By the time a tooth genuinely hurts, it has usually been working on the nerve for a while. Twice-yearly check-ups exist precisely to find this during the boring stage.

5. You do not know where to start

The easiest entry point is a check-up and cleaning. No treatment, no drilling, nothing to be afraid of. If you have been away a long time, say so on the phone when you book and it changes how the appointment is planned.

If you have been putting this off and something in the list was your reason, mention it in your appointment request. It changes how I plan the visit, and it takes about ten seconds.
