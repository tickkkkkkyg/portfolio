Your gums tell you before your teeth do

Bleeding when you brush is not normal, and it is not something to wait out. What it usually means, and what to do about it.

Tooth decay announces itself with pain. Gum disease very often does not. It is one of the few conditions in dentistry where the earliest symptom is the most useful one, and most people treat it as a reason to buy a softer brush.

What is actually happening

Plaque settles along the gum line and mineralises into calculus. The gum becomes inflamed and tightens around the neck of the tooth. When you brush, the swollen tissue bleeds. The bleeding causes people to brush that area more gently, which leaves more plaque, which makes the inflammation worse. It is a loop, and it runs for years without being painful.

What to look for

Blood in the foam when you spit. Gums that look puffy rather than firm. Bad breath that returns within a few hours of brushing. A tooth that suddenly feels slightly longer, because the gum has receded from it. Any one of those is worth an examination.

What to do about it

Do not stop brushing the area. Clean it more thoroughly, not less. Interdental brushes reach below the gum line where a toothbrush cannot, and for most people they matter more than floss. Then book an examination, because the deposits hardened on the root surface need removing and no amount of home care will shift them.

Left untreated, gum disease loosens teeth and eventually costs them. It is one of the few dental problems where the treatment is uncomfortable and the alternative is considerably worse.

If your gums bleed every day, that is a good reason to book an appointment even if nothing else hurts. It is the cheapest problem to catch and the most expensive one to leave alone.
