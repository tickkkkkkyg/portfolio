Why I rewrote my portfolio from scratch

The old one was fine. Fine is the enemy of memorable. Here's what changed and why.

The previous version of this site was competent. It had a hero, a work grid, an about section, and a contact form. It loaded fast and it was accessible. It was also, frankly, forgettable.

Competent is the floor, not the ceiling. When someone lands on your portfolio, they are deciding whether you have taste. A generic template answers that question before they read a word.

What I changed

I swapped the dark-mode SaaS card kit for a warm paper ground and a single indigo-violet accent. I replaced identical rounded cards with an asymmetric grid. I removed the numbered section markers because my sections are not a sequence.

The one place I spent real boldness is the hero: a serif italic accent, a typewriter line, and a particle field that follows the cursor. Everything else got quieter.

The rule I kept

Motion is a statement about hierarchy. If I cannot name what an animation is telling the user, I delete it. That single filter removed most of the scattered fade-and-slide entrances.

The result is a site that feels like one person made it, on purpose, for a reason. That's the only bar I care about.
